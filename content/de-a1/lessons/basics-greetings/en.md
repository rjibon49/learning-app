In Germany you greet people when you enter a shop, a doctor's waiting room or a lift. The right greeting depends on the time of day and on how well you know the person.

## Greetings through the day

| Deutsch | English | When |
|---|---|---|
| Guten Morgen! | Good morning! | until about 11 a.m. |
| Guten Tag! | Good day! / Hello! | during the day, polite |
| Guten Abend! | Good evening! | from about 6 p.m. |
| Gute Nacht! | Good night! | only when going to bed |
| Hallo! | Hello! / Hi! | any time, friendly |
| Hi! | Hi! | young people, very informal |

## Regional greetings

| Deutsch | Where |
|---|---|
| Moin! | North Germany, all day |
| Servus! | Bavaria and Austria (hello and bye) |
| Grüß Gott! | South Germany and Austria |
| Grüezi! | Switzerland |

## How are you?

| Deutsch | English |
|---|---|
| Wie geht es Ihnen? | How are you? (formal) |
| Wie geht's? | How are you? (informal) |
| Sehr gut, danke. | Very good, thanks. |
| Gut, danke. Und Ihnen? | Fine, thanks. And you? (formal) |
| Gut, danke. Und dir? | Fine, thanks. And you? (informal) |
| Es geht. | So-so. |
| Nicht so gut. | Not so good. |

## Formal or informal?

- **Sie** (formal you): strangers, shop staff, teachers, officials → "Guten Tag, Frau Müller. Wie geht es Ihnen?"
- **du** (informal you): friends, family, children, classmates → "Hallo Tom! Wie geht's?"

When you are not sure, use **Sie**. People will tell you "Wir können uns duzen" (we can say du).

## A short dialogue

**Frau Weber:** Guten Morgen, Herr Ali!
**Herr Ali:** Guten Morgen, Frau Weber! Wie geht es Ihnen?
**Frau Weber:** Danke, sehr gut. Und Ihnen?
**Herr Ali:** Auch gut, danke.

> **Tip:** "Gute Nacht" is not a greeting. Say it only when you leave in the evening or go to sleep.
