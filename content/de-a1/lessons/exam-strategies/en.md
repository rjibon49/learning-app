Many learners know enough German to pass — but lose points through stress and small mistakes. These strategies help.

## Before you listen or read

1. **Read the question first.** Then you know what to look for.
2. **Underline key words** in the question: Wann? Wo? Wie viel? Wer?
3. **Think about possible answers.** If the question is "Wann?", you will listen for a time or a day.

## Listening (Hören)

- You won't understand every word. **That's normal.** Listen for the information you need.
- Numbers, times and days are often the answer. Write them down while you listen.
- Watch out for **changes**: "Wir treffen uns um 3 Uhr — nein, lieber um 4." The answer is 4!
- If you hear a text twice: the first time, find the answer; the second time, check it.

## Reading (Lesen)

- Don't read every word slowly. **Look for the key information** (scanning).
- In "richtig/falsch" tasks, check small words: **nicht, kein, nur, immer, nie**.
- Ads: look at **days, times, prices** first.

## Writing (Schreiben)

- Write about **all points** in the task. Each missing point costs points.
- Short, simple sentences are fine. Correct and clear is better than long and wrong.
- Don't forget greeting and ending: "Liebe …," / "Viele Grüße".

## Speaking (Sprechen)

- Speak slowly. Short answers are OK.
- If you don't understand: "Können Sie das bitte wiederholen?"
- It's a group exam: listen to the others and react.

## General

- **Never leave an answer empty.** A guess has a chance; an empty box has none.
- Watch the time. Don't get stuck on one question.

## Useful sentences in the exam

| Deutsch | English |
|---|---|
| Wie bitte? | Pardon? |
| Noch einmal, bitte. | Once more, please. |
| Ich verstehe das Wort nicht. | I don't understand the word. |
| Wie schreibt man das? | How do you write that? |
