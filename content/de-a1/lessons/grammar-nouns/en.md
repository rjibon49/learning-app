Three things are special about German nouns: they start with a **capital letter**, they have a **gender**, and their **plural** has several forms.

## 1. Always a capital letter

Every noun is written with a capital letter — in the middle of a sentence too:

- Ich trinke einen **Kaffee** in der **Küche**.
- Mein **Bruder** hat ein **Auto**.

## 2. Three genders

| Gender | Article | Example |
|---|---|---|
| masculine | der | der Tisch, der Mann |
| feminine | die | die Lampe, die Frau |
| neuter | das | das Buch, das Kind |

The gender of things is often not logical (der Löffel = spoon, die Gabel = fork, das Messer = knife). So always learn a noun **with its article**.

## 3. Plural: always "die"

In the plural every noun takes **die**. The noun itself gets one of these endings:

| Ending | Deutsch (singular) | Deutsch (plural) |
|---|---|---|
| -e | der Tisch | die Tische |
| -¨e | der Stuhl | die Stühle |
| -er / -¨er | das Kind / das Buch | die Kinder / die Bücher |
| -n / -en | die Lampe / die Frau | die Lampen / die Frauen |
| -s | das Auto | die Autos |
| – / -¨ | der Lehrer / die Mutter | die Lehrer / die Mütter |

## Helpful patterns

- Feminine nouns usually add **-n / -en**: die Tasche → die Taschen, die Zeitung → die Zeitungen
- **-in** (female jobs) → **-innen**: die Lehrerin → die Lehrerinnen
- Words from English or ending in a vowel like -a, -o often add **-s**: das Handy → die Handys, das Foto → die Fotos
- Nouns ending in **-er, -el, -en** often stay the same: der Computer → die Computer

> **Tip:** In the app's word list the plural is shown under every noun. Learn three things together: **der Tisch, die Tische**.
