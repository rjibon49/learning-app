German uses the same 26 letters as English, plus **ä, ö, ü** and **ß**. Most letters sound the way they are written, so once you know the alphabet you can read almost any German word.

## Four extra letters

| Deutsch | Name | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ä ä | a-Umlaut | Käse | cheese |
| Ö ö | o-Umlaut | schön | beautiful |
| Ü ü | u-Umlaut | Tür | door |
| ß | eszett | Straße | street |

**ß** is a long, sharp "s". It never starts a word.

## Watch out — letters that sound different from English

- **W** sounds like English **v**: Wasser, Wein
- **V** usually sounds like English **f**: Vater, Vogel
- **J** sounds like English **y**: ja, Junge
- **Z** sounds like **ts**: Zeit, zehn
- **S** before a vowel sounds like English **z**: Sonne, sieben

## Spelling your name

At offices in Germany you are often asked: "Wie schreibt man das?" (How do you spell that?). Practise spelling your name letter by letter:

- Karim: K – A – R – I – M
- Anna: A – N – N – A

> **Tip:** Nouns in German always start with a capital letter: das Haus, der Tisch, die Katze.
