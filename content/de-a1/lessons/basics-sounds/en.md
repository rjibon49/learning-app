A few letter combinations make sounds that English does not have. Learn these and German pronunciation becomes easy.

## Umlauts: ä, ö, ü

| Deutsch | How to say it | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| ä | like "e" in "bed" | Käse | cheese |
| ö | say "e", round your lips | schön | beautiful |
| ü | say "ee", round your lips | müde | tired |

## Letter groups

| Deutsch | Sound | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| ei | "eye" | drei | three |
| ie | long "ee" | Bier | beer |
| eu | "oy" | neu | new |
| äu | "oy" | Häuser | houses |
| au | "ow" | Haus | house |
| sch | "sh" | Schule | school |
| sp | "shp" at the start of a word | Sport | sport |
| st | "sht" at the start of a word | Stadt | city |
| ch | after a, o, u: throat sound, like Scottish "loch" | Buch | book |
| ch | after e, i, ä, ö, ü: soft hiss, like "h" in "huge" | ich | I |
| tsch | "ch" in "chair" | Deutsch | German |
| pf | p + f together | Apfel | apple |
| qu | "kv" | Quatsch | nonsense |

## The famous pair: ei and ie

Read the **second** letter in English:

- **ei** → say "i" → "eye": mein, Wein, drei
- **ie** → say "e" → "ee": Bier, vier, sieben

## Endings

- **-er** at the end sounds almost like "a": Vater, Lehrer, Wasser
- **-b, -d, -g** at the end sound like **p, t, k**: gelb, Hund, Tag

## Long and short vowels

- Double vowel or **h** after a vowel = long: Tee, Boot, Zahn, Uhr
- Double consonant after a vowel = short: Bett, Mutter, kommen

> **Tip:** Say "ich" with a smile and "Buch" from the back of your throat. That is the whole secret of the German "ch".
